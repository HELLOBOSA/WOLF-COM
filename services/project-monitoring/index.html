Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between project monitoring and project management?

Monitoring makes the project visible. It organises current status, dated observations, visual records, open questions and decisions into one project record. Project Management directs the work itself: brief, consultants, procurement, programme, budget and change coordination. Monitoring reports on the project, management runs it. The two can be appointed together or separately.

Can I follow the project if I live in another country?

That is the situation the service is built around. Status, dated site photographs, open points and the decisions waiting on you are kept in one place, so following the project does not depend on being present or on gathering information from separate message threads.

Who can see the project space?

Only the recipients agreed for the commission. The space is private and password-protected, and the list of people with access is set when the reporting framework is defined.

How often are updates published?

The rhythm is agreed at the start rather than fixed in advance, because it follows the phase and the pace of the work. Updates are tied to agreed visits and to the information available at that point, so each one reflects real change rather than a scheduled entry with nothing to report.

Is monitoring included in every commission?

No. It applies on selected commissions and only when expressly included in the written appointment. Observations are based on visible conditions and on the documents made available at the time, which is also why the scope is agreed in writing before the first update.
